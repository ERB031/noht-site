There's a certain kind of energy you feel when you drive through Atlanta. The smells, and sounds, and tastes of this city are unique and distinct. It's a growing and changing place but also one I've grown with as a native ATLian and creative.  Atlanta's creative scene isn't "emerging." The truth is this city has been building something for years. A creative ecosystem that doesn't look or sound like anywhere else. And that's exactly the point.

If you've spent any time in LA or New York's creative industries, you know the playbook. There's a way things are supposed to look, a hierarchy of who gets to tell stories and how. Atlanta has never really cared about that playbook.

Here, [a rapper becomes a showrunner.](https://www.hulu.com/series/atlanta-3529d0f1-acaf-476d-8be3-66cd6bf12530) [A man can go from homeless to become a film titan. ](https://www.vibe.com/news/entertainment/tyler-perry-first-apartment-homeless-evicted-reaction-1234904243/)[A photographer who started shooting friends in a parking lot ends up directing campaigns for national brands.](https://discoveratlanta.com/stories/john-fulton/) The paths aren't linear, and nobody's waiting for an invitation.

That energy isn't something we discovered. It's something we grew up in. NØHT was founded in part by Atlanta natives, and this city's creative DNA is in ours. We didn't move here chasing a trend. We built here because this is home, and we've watched it become what it is from the inside.

One of the most interesting tensions in Atlanta's creative world is the relationship between art and business. This city is home to Tyler Perry Studios, one of the largest film production facilities in the country. One that's employed several of our founders.  It's also home to underground collectives making experimental short films on shoestring budgets in somebody's living room. What makes Atlanta different is that those worlds aren't as separate as you'd think. The same DP who shoots a commercial for a Fortune 500 brand might spend her weekend collaborating on an indie music video or independent short film. The same editor cutting a nonprofit's annual campaign film might also be finishing his feature that'll screen at a festival next spring.

Our own team is proof of that fluidity. NØHT's members have worked across documentary, union productions, indie film, and music video. Sometimes all in the same week. That range isn't a coincidence. It's what happens when you come up in a city that doesn't believe in creative silos. We carry that experience into everything we do, whether it's brand videos, nonprofit storytelling, or commercial work, because the craft doesn't change just because the context does. A frame is a frame. A story is a story. The care you bring to it is what separates forgettable content from something that actually lands.

We're based right in downtown in the heart of the city and that energy fuels our creative and business decisions.  We are Atlanta and we are inspired by Atlanta.  Working with creatives who are embedded in a scene like this means your project benefits from a sensibility that's hard to manufacture. There's a texture to work that comes out of Atlanta, a warmth, a directness, a visual confidence, that you can feel even if you can't name it.

We've seen it in our own work. When we produce a brand video for a local company, it doesn't feel like a template. It feels like it belongs here. When we tell a nonprofit's story, the authenticity isn't a strategy. It's just how we shoot, because it's how we see.

Atlanta isn't trying to become the next anything. The creatives here, the filmmakers, the musicians, the designers, the founders, are building something for here and from here.  If you're in Atlanta and you're making something, we see you. And if you're outside Atlanta looking in, wondering what's happening here, come find out. The city's not waiting. Neither should you.
